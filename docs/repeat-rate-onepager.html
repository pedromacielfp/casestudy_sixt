Growth & CRM  /  Experiment brief

Converting the 70%

Three CRM experiments to move Sixt US leisure customers from one‑time to repeat — each tied to a signal on the conversion dashboard.

Source  Sixt CRM Dashboard (mock data) North‑star  Core Repeat Conversion Rate Status  For review

The US leisure book grows double digits year over year, but 70% of customers rent once and never return — 2,800 of the 4,000 in the current dataset, split exactly 50/50 between the direct (B2C) and partner (B2P) channels. The Core Repeat Conversion Rate runs a few points above its 30% lifetime baseline and is trending up (32.7% year‑to‑date 2026; 34.5% in 2024, 36.6% in 2025). Each experiment below targets a specific gap the dashboard surfaces. They are ordered by expected impact on that rate, and Initiatives 1 and 3 share one enrolled population so they compound.

01

Put every one‑time renter into a post‑rental retention flow

The bet: a light, well‑timed sequence after the trip keeps Sixt in the consideration set for the next one.

Flagged in the dashboard
Marketing Engagement. Customers who engaged with a retention campaign repeat at ~40% versus ~20% for those who did not — and only 39% of B2P customers are reached at all, against 64% of B2C. The single largest unworked lever on the page.
Hypothesis

If a completed one‑time rental is enrolled within 7 days into a four‑touch flow (thank‑you → destination tips → day‑75 “ready for your next trip” → win‑back at day 200), then its probability of a second rental within 365 days rises by ≥ 3 pp, because the barrier for most one‑timers is recall at the moment of the next trip decision, not intent.

A/B test
Unit
Individual customer, at rental completion.
Split
50 / 50, assigned at return. Continuous enrolment.
Treatment
Enrolled in the retention flow.
Control
Transactional messages only (receipt, damage, survey).
CRM channels
Email (primary) · Push (if app installed) · SMS (day‑75 touch only)
Readout
Cohorts measured at 90 / 180 / 365 days.
Success metric
PrimaryRepeat conversion rate at 365 days, treatment vs control. Ship on ≥ 2 pp absolute lift, 95% confidence.
SecondaryTime‑to‑second‑rental; rentals per customer at 365 days.
GuardrailUnsubscribe < 0.5% per send; contribution margin per rental flat or up.
Impact on the goal High — direct The primary metric is the north‑star. A 3 pp lift across the enrolled base moves the blended rate ~2 pp.
02

Move B2P (broker) customers onto a direct relationship

The bet: B2P customers don’t come back because their next booking sits with the broker, not Sixt — give them a reason to book direct once.

Flagged in the dashboard
Channel Breakdown. B2P repeats at 21% against B2C’s 42%. The book acquires the same number of one‑time customers through each channel (~1,400), but B2P is 80% one‑time to B2C’s 62% — same cost of acquisition, far worse retention, and the largest single drag on the blended rate.
Hypothesis

If a B2P customer is prompted at pickup or return to create a direct Sixt account and given a bounded incentive on their next booking on sixt.com (€25 off, expires in 6 months), then their 365‑day repeat rate moves a third of the way toward B2C (~20% → ~26%), because a first direct booking establishes the account, the payment method and the marketing consent that a broker booking never creates.

A/B test
Unit
B2P rental, randomised at pickup.
Split
50 / 50, stratified by broker partner.
Treatment
Account‑creation prompt + next‑direct‑booking incentive.
Control
Standard B2P experience, no prompt.
CRM channels
Email + SMS (broker customers rarely have the app) · QR prompt on the rental agreement
Readout
180 and 365 days; funnel checked weekly.
Success metric
Primary365‑day repeat conversion rate of treated B2P customers vs control.
FunnelDirect‑account creation rate → next rental books direct → incentive redemption.
GuardrailIncremental margin per converted customer positive after incentive cost; control isolates customers who would have rebooked anyway.
Impact on the goal High Closing a third of the B2C–B2P gap across the ~1,750 B2P customers lifts the blended Core Repeat Conversion Rate ~2–3 pp.
03

Time re‑engagement to the two natural rebooking windows

The bet: a message that lands inside a trip‑planning window converts far better than a fixed monthly newsletter.

Flagged in the dashboard
Cohort & Frequency. Time‑to‑next‑rental has two clean clusters — 60–90 days (frequent renters) and ~330–365 days (annual‑trip renters). The cohort matrix also shows October–December acquisitions rebooking at near zero.
Hypothesis

If the generic monthly newsletter is replaced by two behaviour‑timed triggers — a “next trip?” nudge at day 75 and a “book your summer trip again” reminder at month 11 for summer renters — then second‑rental rate rises, most for the Q4‑acquired cohort, because the send now coincides with when the customer is actually choosing their next rental.

A/B test
Population
Within Initiative 1’s enrolled group.
Split
50 / 50, stratified by acquisition month (Jun–Aug vs Oct–Dec).
Treatment
Cadence‑timed triggers, replacing the newsletter.
Control
Generic monthly newsletter.
CRM channels
Day‑75: Email + SMS + Push · Month‑11: Email + Push
Readout
120 days (frequent arm), 400 days (seasonal arm).
Success metric
PrimarySecond‑rental rate at 120 days (frequent) and 400 days (seasonal) vs control.
EfficiencyIncremental repeat conversions per 1,000 sends; send volume neutral or lower.
SegmentRepeat‑rate lift for the Oct–Dec acquisition cohort specifically.
Impact on the goal Medium — compounding Smaller standalone effect, but it multiplies Initiative 1 (same population, better timing) and rescues the near‑zero Q4 cohort.

How every test is measured

  • North‑starCore Repeat Conversion Rate — unique customers in an acquisition cohort with a 2nd‑plus lifetime rental ÷ cohort size — read at fixed horizons 90 / 180 / 365 / 400 days because rebooking is bimodal.
  • DesignRandomised holdout, never pre / post. Seasonality and the double‑digit growth trend would otherwise contaminate the read.
  • IncrementalityA permanent 10% global control, refreshed yearly, measures the whole programme against no CRM contact.
  • Decision ruleShip when the 365‑day repeat‑rate lift is ≥ +2 pp absolute at 95% confidence and contribution margin per customer is not down.
  • GuardrailsOpt‑out rate, spam‑complaint rate, margin per rental, and send fatigue (open / click‑through trend) tracked on every arm.
  • PowerDetecting +2 pp on a ~20% base needs ~6,500 customers per arm (80% power, 95% conf). Initiatives 1 & 3 reach that in ~1 quarter at production volume; Initiative 2 (B2P only) in ~4–5 months.

Sequencing Run 1 and 2 in parallel (different populations). Start 3 once Initiative 1 has a stable enrolled base (~1 month in).
